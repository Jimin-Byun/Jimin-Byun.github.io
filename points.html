What the Points Keep

A dance phrase passed through a pose-tracking system and read through four lenses. Each lens shows what the machine receives from the moving body, what it can compute back, and what it leaves out.

Question

When a dancer’s movement becomes data, which qualities survive the translation, and which disappear?

What this study shows

A summary of what the pose data kept and lost in this duet.

Carried over

  • RhythmWhen a movement turns sudden the Time value rises, and when it slows the value falls.
  • ReachThe Residue lens draws how far each dancer reaches around the body.

Not recorded

  • BreathThere are no points on the chest or back, so the widening of the ribs leaves no trace.
  • IntentionA reach toward a partner and a push away, on the same path at the same speed, get the same values.

Limits

  • Five pointsEffort and the traces use only the head, wrists and ankles; the hands and feet themselves are not tracked.
  • The liftFrom 15.4 to 21.9 seconds one dancer is hidden by the other and drops out of the data.

What the data cannot hold

The four values follow the speed, acceleration, jerk and path of five points. Changes of tempo and suddenness register in them, and the Quality lens makes them visible.

What they cannot register is why a movement is made. A reach toward someone and a push away, along the same path at the same speed, receive the same values. Maxine Sheets-Johnstone (1999) describes movement as a way of thinking that is felt from inside; the data has no record of that inside.

In doctoral research I want to study where and how such qualities are lost or changed as movement passes through motion capture, avatars and AI systems.
